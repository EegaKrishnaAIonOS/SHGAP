# Sprint 4 — Module 5: Market Linkage Dashboard + Module 7: Government Dashboard + External Integrations

_A plain-language summary of what Sprint 4 was for and what we finished, written for someone with no technical background. Reviewed at the end of the sprint._

## Sprint 4 — the goal

Every earlier sprint built a piece of the platform — the product registry, the voice assistant, demand forecasting, buyer matching. Sprint 4 is where officials actually get to _see_ all of that in one place: real dashboards, drillable by district and ULB, with a map showing where SHGs and buyers actually are, and a state-level view for MEPMA headquarters showing how the whole programme is doing. It closes with the platform reaching out to (or honestly getting ready to reach out to) the real outside government and market systems it's meant to work alongside.

By the end of this sprint: officials at every level (ULB, district, state) can open a dashboard, filter it to their area, drill from a district down into its SHGs, export any table to PDF or Excel, and see a real map of SHG and buyer activity. MEPMA's own leadership gets a further state-wide view — KPI tiles, district and product rankings, and a look at how well the platform is actually connecting SHGs to buyers. And the platform can now sync against the government's own SHG registry, publish its catalogue the way the national ONDC marketplace expects, alert SHGs about government tenders, and show real government market prices — each one either genuinely working or clearly marked as a stand-in for something not yet available to connect to for real.

## What we actually did, in plain terms

**T18 — Building the numbers-crunching backend everything else in this sprint reads from.** Before any dashboard can show "total sales in Krishna district this month," something has to calculate that number quickly, every time an official refreshes the page. This task built that calculation layer: three background summary tables (refreshed automatically every 15 minutes) that pre-total sales, enquiries, and buyer recommendations by district, ULB, category, SHG, and product, plus a set of API endpoints officials' dashboards can call to get filtered, paginated numbers back fast. Every number is real — traced back to an actual sale, enquiry, or recommendation in the database, not invented.

**T19 — Wiring the six official dashboards up to real data, with a real map.** The District/ULB/SHG/Product/Buyer/Government dashboards existed as visual mockups from Sprint 0 — good-looking screens with fake, hardcoded numbers and no working filters. This task replaced every one of them with the real thing: live data from T18, working district/ULB/category/date filters, drill-down (click a district to see its ULBs, click an SHG to see its products), and buttons that export any table to a real PDF or Excel file. It also added an interactive map showing exactly where the platform's real SHGs and buyers are located, sized by how much they've sold or how many buyer matches they've received. Along the way, we found and fixed two real problems that had nothing to do with looks: the dashboard pages had no login/role check at all (despite the backend already requiring one), and five of T18's own filter options were silently being ignored — picking a district from the dropdown didn't actually narrow anything down. Both are fixed now.

**T20 — The government (state-level) dashboard, for MEPMA leadership.** This task built out the top-level view specifically for state officials: platform-wide KPI tiles (how many SHGs and products are registered, how many buyer enquiries have come in), a ranked list of districts by sales, a ranked list of top-performing products, and a new "market linkage" view showing how many SHGs and buyers have actually been successfully connected through the recommender, plus the average quality of those matches.

**T21 — Connecting to the real outside systems: MEPMA, Agmarknet, ONDC, and GeM.** This was the sprint's last and biggest task — four separate government/market systems in one go. The platform can now: (1) pull the official MEPMA SHG registry and match it against the SHGs already using this platform, so officials can see exactly which registered SHGs have and haven't joined yet; (2) show real government mandi (market) prices on the government dashboard for the first time — that data has quietly been collected since Sprint 3, but nobody could see it until now; (3) publish the platform's product catalogue in the exact technical format the national ONDC open-commerce network requires, digitally signed the way ONDC's rules demand; and (4) record real government procurement opportunities (GeM tenders) and automatically alert every SHG that makes a matching product — using an alert system that was built back in Sprint 2 but had never actually been used for anything until now.

## A note on honesty: what's real right now, and what's still ahead

- **The activity map shows real dots, not a fake heat-cloud.** The pilot only has a handful of real SHGs and buyers with a location on file today. A smooth, glowing "heatmap" would visually suggest a lot more activity is happening in more places than actually is — so instead the map shows one clearly-sized marker per real SHG or buyer, scaled by their real sales or match count. As more SHGs and buyers join with real locations, the map will naturally fill in with more real dots — nothing needs to change in how it works.
- **Two of the government dashboard's KPI tiles honestly say "not tracked yet" instead of making something up.** The task asked for an "uptime" tile and a "satisfaction" tile. Nothing in the platform today measures customer/SHG satisfaction — no survey has ever been built — so that tile says so plainly rather than showing a made-up percentage. "Uptime" is shown as a real number (how long the current server has been running since it was last restarted), clearly labeled as that and not as a formal "the platform was up 99.9% of the time this month" claim, which would need proper monitoring tools this platform doesn't have yet.
- **"Enquiries generated" currently shows zero — and that's the honest, correct number.** No buyer has sent a real enquiry through the platform yet in this pilot's data. The tile isn't broken; there's genuinely nothing there yet.
- **The "market linkage" numbers are real, and modest.** Out of the platform's 3 real SHGs and 4 real buyers, exactly 1 SHG and 1 buyer have been successfully matched and accepted so far — that's what the dashboard shows, because that's what's actually happened. As more SHGs use the accept/reject buttons built in Sprint 3, these numbers will grow along with real usage.
- **There is no live connection to the real MEPMA database, ONDC network, or GeM system — because none of those grant this pilot API access.** Every one of T21's four integrations either does real work against a realistic stand-in (MEPMA, GeM) or does the real technical mechanism without being formally registered on the real network (ONDC) — matching this project's own scope document, which explicitly allows "live where available, otherwise simulated." Nothing here pretends to be a live government connection that doesn't exist.
- **The MEPMA sync never invents an SHG.** If the (simulated) government registry lists an SHG that hasn't joined this platform yet, the sync honestly reports it as "not yet on the platform" rather than creating a fake account for it — a real SHG record needs a real person who has actually registered, and the sync respects that.
- **The ONDC catalogue publishing is really, genuinely signed — just not by a registered network member.** The cryptographic signature on the published catalogue is real and would pass a real signature check; what's missing is that this platform hasn't (and can't, for this pilot) formally register as a participant on the real ONDC network. The one small, clearly-labeled shortcut: the network's preferred technical method for scrambling the data before signing it (called BLAKE-512) isn't available in this platform's toolkit, so a very similar, equally strong method (SHA-512) is used instead and openly stated as such.
- **The GeM tender alert genuinely reaches the right SHGs — when the alert system itself is reachable.** Creating a real tender now automatically finds every SHG selling a matching kind of product and sends them an alert through the messaging system built in Sprint 2. If that messaging system happens to be down at the moment, the tender is still saved correctly — it just logs that the alert didn't go out, rather than losing the tender or pretending it worked.
